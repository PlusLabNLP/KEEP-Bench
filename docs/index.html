Abstract

Context compression can introduce faithfulness issues through an information bottleneck, and QA metrics miss them. KEEP-Bench checks the compressed context itself, on 4,102 cases that require keeping plausible reference resolutions, explicit qualifiers and minority perspectives.

Read the full abstract

Context compression improves long-context efficiency but may introduce faithfulness issues through an information bottleneck. Existing evaluations rely on QA metrics, but a reader can still answer correctly from an unfaithful compression, so these metrics underestimate such loss. To bridge this gap, we introduce KEEP-Bench, a diagnostic benchmark that directly assesses compression faithfulness with 4,102 cases across three challenging settings that require keeping evidence for plausible reference resolutions, explicit qualifiers, and minority perspectives.

Experiments show that even with tailored prompting, the strongest models remain unreliable: despite context windows exceeding 100K tokens and inputs averaging only 3.1K tokens, the best open-weight model (31B) preserves all required evidence in only 66.8% of cases, and the best proprietary model in only 61.6%. Performance also varies sharply across evidence structures, so conclusions from limited structures may be biased.

We further introduce a metric, Evidence Preservation Return (EPR), to relate evidence recall to inference FLOPs, and find that it peaks at moderate scales (4B–8B), where recall remains low. Additional output budget mainly benefits already strong models and widens capability gaps, yet the stronger preservation of these models comes at a steep computational cost.

These findings highlight the importance for future work to investigate compressors with strong information preservation under a small computation budget, rather than relying on a large output budget or model scaling.

Keep both interpretations

Underspecified mentions admit two valid interpretations, each with its own reasoning chain, so a faithful compression must keep both.

900 cases, two to four hops, built from MuSiQue with fictional entities so lost evidence cannot be recalled from memory.

Para-Rec: the evidence that separates the two interpretations survives. All-Rec: every proposition in both chains survives.

Main results

No compressor reliably keeps all the evidence

The best model keeps every proposition in only 66.8% of cases, and the leader changes with the structure: Claude-Haiku-4.5 is first on parallel evidence but trails GPT-5.4-mini on science.

Compute

No compressor is both cheap and faithful

Larger models keep more evidence, but compute grows faster than recall, so EPR peaks at moderate sizes. The cheapest method, RECOMP (extractive), keeps all evidence in under 5% of cases.

Each point is one method. Points on a dashed curve share an EPR; up and to the left is better.
